### Traficom: Cybermeter - User guide
#### Summary and checklist of the assessment process :
1. **Assesment Iniatiation** with objective declaration and appointment of sponsor and facilitator.
2. **Preparation for Assesment** includes definition of operating areas and critical dependencies,identification of specialists and methods & schedule.
3. **Assesment is conducted** using Cybermeter self-assesment tool.
4. Analysis of assesment results into the **identification of development activities**.
5. Organistaion carries out hte **develoment activities** and updates the **results**.

#### Cybermeter maturity model :
- Cybermeter:Maturity model designed for assesment and development of Cybersecurity capabilities at 4 levels
- Level 0: No basic requirements are met
- Level 1: Ad-hoc requirementys are met.
- Level 2: Cybersecuirty activities are documented,sufficient skills with defined roles.
- Level 3: More advanced and steered by orgainsation's policies

##### Key Concepts:
- **Capability**: Correct operation using skills and rrsources to achieve objectives in a specific area.
- **Maturity Model**:Activities are examined at levels to achieve systematic and advanced version.
- **Operating Area**: Critical services and functions which are being assessed.

##### Domains, objectives, and practices:
- The framework exmaines 11 **domains**:CRITICAL,RISK,DEPENDENCIES,ASSET,ACCESS,THREAT,SITUATION,RESPONSE,WORKFORCE,ARCHITECTURE and PROGRAM.
- **Objectives**: 52 objectives cover the cybermeter domain. They either represent **Cybersecurity domain** or **General management objectives**.
- **Practices**: The practices represent standard and effective cybersecurity measures that organisations in different industries routinely apply.

##### Maturity indicator level calculation model:
- Three-Stage Calculation Process
1. Assess each practice using four reponse options:Not implemented/Partly implemented/Mostly implemented/Completely implemented.
2. Calculate maturity level for each objective
- Level 1: All (100%) level-1 practices must be implemented.
- Level 2:More than half (>50%) of level-2 practices, and all level-1 practices.
- Level 3:More than half (>50%) of level-3 practices, and all level-1 and level-2 practices.
3. Calculate maturity level for each domain: The domain’s maturity level = the lowest maturity level among its objectives.

- Domain-specific maturity:Each domain is assessed independently; organisations may score differently across domains.
- Cumulative progression:You cannot reach a higher maturity level until all required lower-level practices are fully implemented.
- Target levels must fit context: Organisations should set target maturity levels per domain based on their current state,business goals,risk profile and sector recommendations.

#### The Cybermeter maturity report:
- The Cybermeter maturity report presents detailed cybersecurity assessment results for 11 domains.
- It is mainly for cybersecurity and risk management professionals.

### Traficom: Kybermittari spreadsheet:
- Kybermittari is a tool from Traficom/NCSC-FI for assessing an organisation’s cybersecurity maturity.
- It covers 11 cybersecurity domains and gives a maturity level (0–3) for each.
- I went through the following sections:
-  **Critical** which identifies the critical services,their associated risks and it's management.
-  **Threat** includes plans, procedures, and tools to detect, identify, analyze, and respond to cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities.
-  **Risk** is part of organisation's overall enterprise risk management which identifies,analyses and responds to cyber risks.
-  **Response** are the plans and procedures to recover from cybersecurity events.

## b) Sucks or succeeds:
**How Kybermittari could be useless:**
- If an organisation just fills the spreadsheet without honest assessment, results are meaningless.
- If the staff doesn’t understand the definitions of threats, vulnerabilities, or practices, the maturity levels will be inaccurate.
- Overly rigid scoring could ignore real-life context. (Rochford,2017)

**How Kybermittari could be really useful:**
- Provides a structured overview of strengths and weaknesses across 11 domains.
- Encourages cross-department discussion (IT, operations, management).
- Helps track progress over time and supports prioritization of improvements.(Fraktal, n.d.)

## c) Competition:
I read about the following tools which are used for cybersecurity maturity assessment.

- **C2M2 (Cybersecurity Capability Maturity Model):** Detailed, widely recognized, industry-standard; all practices must be implemented for each level → very rigorous.
  Downside: Can be too strict and heavy for smaller organisations; slower to show progress.(Flexential,2025)

- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF):** Flexible, widely adopted, aligns cybersecurity with business objectives.
  Downside: Not a spreadsheet-style maturity tool; may require additional effort to quantify levels across domains.(Flexential,2025)

 **Comparison:** Kybermittari is lighter and easier for Finnish organisations to implement; it highlights progress even if a few practices are missing, which can be motivating.
