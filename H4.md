## X) Why would you want to use a feed reader (and RSS/Atom)?

I haven't been using a feed reader before this assignment(just had a glimps of it in my college days),but when researching about it's benefits and advantages,i came across some nice articles. The main points i found specially advantageous are listed below:

 - **All updates in one place**: A feed reader aggregates new posts from all your chosen sites.

- **No algorithms**: You see every post in chronological order — nothing hidden or prioritized for engagement.

- **Privacy-friendly**: No tracking, profiling, or the need for accounts.

- **Cleaner experience**: Most readers strip away ads, pop-ups, and clutter.

- **Real-time updates**: New content appears immediately.

- **Supports many media types**: Blogs, news, podcasts, videos, and more.

- **Customizable**: You control exactly what sources and topics you follow.
 - **Offline access**: Many readers let you download articles to read later.
   
## y) Supreme court of Finland, case Osuuspankkikeskus port scan KKO:2003:36:
I looked at the Supreme Court (Korkein oikeus) decision,honestly it was a bit difficult for me to follow because of the legal langauage.Here are the key points:

- A 17-year-old (“A”) used a port-scanning tool in 1998 to scan the OP Bank Centre’s network for open proxy servers.

- The scan did not break through the firewall, but it systematically probed every address in the bank’s network.

### Lower Court:

- Accepted evidence that the scan came from A’s computer, but said it might have been someone else.

- Dismissed the attempted data-breach charge and all related damages.

### Court of Appeal:

- Found it proven that A performed the scan.

- Said port scanning with the intent to exploit any discovered weakness is an attempted data breach.

- Ordered A to pay FIM 75,000 in total damages for investigative and expert-analysis costs.

### Supreme Court:
Upheld the Court of Appeal.

 Ruled that:

- Port scanning is a method of looking for weaknesses that enable intrusion.

- The specialised scanning software showed intent to access the system unlawfully.

- A’s explanation (“just looking for open proxies”) was not credible.

Damages were not reduced, even though A was a minor, because he was technically skilled and understood the risks.

### Core finding:
Port scanning aimed at discovering vulnerabilities counts as an attempted data breach, and organisations may claim full compensation for the security investigations that follow.
